# Mission 3 Reflection

## My Multi-Cloud Explorer Experience

Completing Mission 3 gave me a better understanding of why organizations need to evaluate cloud providers based on their actual requirements instead of simply selecting the most popular platform. Among AWS, Microsoft Azure, and Google Cloud, the platform that impressed me the most was Google Cloud because of its strong capabilities in artificial intelligence, machine learning, data analytics, and Kubernetes. I also found AWS impressive because of its broad range of cloud services and global infrastructure.

AWS, Azure, and Google Cloud have many similarities. All three provide virtual machines, object storage, networking, databases, identity and access management, Kubernetes, monitoring, security, and other services. They also use similar cloud concepts such as regions, scalable resources, managed services, and pay-as-you-use cloud models. However, each provider has different strengths and ecosystems that can make one platform more appropriate for a particular organization.

Business requirements have a major influence on cloud platform selection. For example, a university that already uses Windows Server, Microsoft 365, and Active Directory would benefit from Azure because of its Microsoft integration. An AI research company may prefer Google Cloud because of its AI and machine learning capabilities. A startup may choose AWS because of its broad selection of services and scalability.

Acting as a Cloud Solutions Consultant taught me that cloud computing decisions should be based on technical requirements and business goals. I learned that it is important to compare services, infrastructure, scalability, integration, and potential use cases before making a recommendation.
